AI & Chatbots

Let AI answer your customers 24/7 — trained on your own content, with smooth human handover when it's needed.

AI providers

Chatbots need an AI engine behind them. Under AI → Providers you connect a provider account and choose the models used for chat and embeddings (used for knowledge-base search).

1

Open AI → Providers and click Add Provider.

2

Pick a provider (for example OpenAI or Google Gemini) and paste your API key.

3

Set the chat model — auto is recommended; the platform then routes to the best healthy model automatically.

4

Enable it and run the built-in diagnostic to confirm everything is wired correctly.

📎 Keep a fallback

Add at least one embed-capable provider. Without embeddings your chatbot falls back to keyword search only, which can miss answers.

Knowledge bases

A knowledge base (KB) is your bot's brain: paste or upload your content and the bot answers from it instead of making things up.

1

Create a KB under AI → Knowledge Bases (name it after its purpose, e.g. “Store FAQ”).

2

Add documents: paste text, upload files, or import FAQ/question-answer content. Every document is automatically split into chunks and indexed.

3

After editing a document, re-index it (re-save or use the re-index action) so the search index refreshes.

💡 What makes a good KB

Write the way customers ask: short Q&A pairs, product names spelled out, prices, policies, opening hours. The bot can only answer what's actually in the KB — generic company questions need the company info written there too.

Creating a chatbot

1

Open AI → Chatbots and click New Chatbot.

2

Give it a name and a system prompt — its personality and rules, e.g. “You are the assistant for X Store. Be friendly, answer in the customer's language, only promise what's in the knowledge base.”

3

Attach a knowledge base — this is what grounds its answers in your content.

4

Set the handover reply — the message sent when a customer asks for a human (translated into 17 languages automatically if left empty).

5

Activate the chatbot. It now answers inbound messages on the channels it's attached to.

Memories

AI → Memories shows what the bot has learned about individual customers — preferences and facts it picked up from conversations. Use it to review what the AI remembers and remove anything that shouldn't be stored.

Testing in the playground

Every chatbot has a playground — a private chat where you test before going live:

  • Ask questions your customers would ask and check the answers.
  • The playground remembers the conversation, so follow-up questions work like a real chat.
  • Try edge cases: a question not in the KB, a request for a human, an angry message with emojis.
💡 Bot ignoring your KB?

Test with a question that literally appears in your KB text. If the playground still answers poorly, re-index your documents and run the provider diagnostic — the usual culprits are a missing embed provider or an unindexed document.

Human handover

When a customer says “talk to a human” (or the AI detects strong frustration):

  1. The bot instantly acknowledges with your handover message.
  2. The conversation is labelled “Waiting for you” in the inbox.
  3. The bot goes quiet — a human agent takes over and the label clears on the first agent reply.

You can also hand over manually from any automation with the Assign agent node, or from the inbox.

Monitoring AI usage

Open Reports → AI to see chatbot activity and usage over time — helpful for understanding volume and spotting cost spikes. See the Reports guide.
